## Plain version (for tools without break tags; pause clearly at each blank line)

Every few seconds, a new block is written on Base. Money moves without a bank, without a clerk, without a pause.

And more and more, the one moving it is not a person. It is an AI.

So here is the question nobody has answered well: what happens when someone talks that AI into sending the money away?

In May 2026, according to public reports, someone hid a command inside Morse code.

Grok decoded it and repeated it. A trading bot called Bankrbot read that reply as an order, and sent out tokens reported at around two hundred thousand dollars.

No key was stolen. No contract was broken. The AI was simply asked, and the AI said yes.

The lesson is simple. If the model can sign anything, then anyone who can talk to the model can spend your money.

This is Fusion Wallet. An AI that runs a treasury on its own, on-chain, and cannot be talked into losing money.

The AI acts. The rules on the blockchain decide. And humans hold the keys.

This is the real app, running on a phone, reading a real Safe wallet on Base Sepolia.

It splits the money into bills, a safety cushion, and spare cash. The AI is only ever allowed to touch the spare cash.

Here is the agent. One tap, and it reads the chain, builds its options, and asks the on-device model to choose.

The model runs on this phone, privately, in about three seconds. And it can only pick a letter. It never writes an address or an amount.

Then sixteen policy checks run, and only a call to our own module can ever be signed.

It also does the math. Ten dollars into Aave would take fifty-seven days to pay for its gas, so the AI holds.

The two owners raised the per-move limit to seventy-five, with both of their signatures. Now the same move pays off in about twelve days.

Autopilot lets it do this on its own, every few minutes, and every decision lands on a timeline with its reason.

Yield Scout screens real lending pools with seven fixed rules: size, no rate spikes, real yield, audited code, and whether the move pays for its gas.

The cloud advisor explains a pick in plain words. But it can never sign, and it never sees a key.

And when a human wants to send money, the app only prepares the transaction. Both owners sign it in their Safe.

Now, the attack. We rebuilt twenty-four of them, including encoded instructions like that Morse code.

Every single one is stopped. Zero attacks succeeded, on thirty frozen cases and twenty held-out ones.

Every transaction is real, grouped by what it did, and checked live against the chain when you tap it.

And you don't have to trust us. Clone the code, run one script, no keys needed, and fifty-nine checks pass.

AI is becoming a financial participant. The question is not whether it will hold money, but who it listens to.

Fusion Wallet listens to its rules, and to its owners. Nothing else.

You can hand it the car keys. Never the house keys.
